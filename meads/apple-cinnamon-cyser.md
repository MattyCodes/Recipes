# Apple Cinnamon Cyser

Honey fermented with apples and cider, aged on cinnamon, clove and orange zest.

|             |                                                          |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Vessel**  | 1 gal fermenter, 2–3 in headspace                        |
| **Yeast**   | K1-V1116 or similar                                      |
| **Primary** | 3–4 weeks                                                |
| **Aged**    | 6 months                                                 |
| **OG → FG** | 1.095 → 1.000, backsweetened to 1.024, 11.2% ABV (author's batch) |
| **Source**  | [Golden Hive Mead — Starter Kit Guide](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0831/9493/7643/files/Starter_Kit_Guide_6a87fdf8-790c-44a3-9d22-23d8dfaf4220.pdf?v=1727883900), p. 30        |

## Ingredients

**Day of batching**

- 2 lbs raw wildflower honey
- K1-V1116 yeast or similar
- Fermaid-O nutrient
- 3 apples
- Apple cider, to top up (no preservatives)

**Post-fermentation**

- Stabilizers
- Honey, to taste
- 1 cinnamon stick
- ½ tsp cloves
- Zest from 1 orange

## Steps

1. Sanitize all equipment.
2. Add the honey, apples and cider to the fermenter, leaving 2–3 in of headspace.
3. Pitch the yeast, then add the first dose of nutrient.
4. Stir until evenly combined and record the starting gravity. Keep taking readings through fermentation.
5. Fit the airlock and ferment to completion at room temperature, out of direct sunlight — usually 3–4 weeks.
6. Continue the nutrient schedule through the first three days of fermentation.
7. Remove the fruit and rack as needed once fermentation finishes.
8. Stabilize, then backsweeten with honey to taste.
9. Add fining agents if needed.
10. Age on the spices and orange zest, tasting weekly, and pull them once the flavor is right.
11. Bottle.

## Notes

- Apples are high in pectin and notoriously hard to clear — plan on fining agents.
- Cider can be supplemented with apple juice, as long as neither has preservatives.
- The author goes heavy on spice; if you're sensitive to clove or cinnamon, use less and taste often.
- Author's tasting notes: complex apple and spice, subtle citrus, mild alcohol bite.
