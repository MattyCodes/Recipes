# Traditional Mead

Honey, water and yeast, nothing else — the baseline every other mead departs from.

|             |                                                          |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Vessel**  | 1 gal fermenter, 2–3 in headspace                        |
| **Yeast**   | K1-V1116 or similar                                      |
| **Primary** | 3–4 weeks                                                |
| **Aged**    | 6 months                                                 |
| **OG → FG** | 1.112 → 1.002, 14.4% ABV (author's batch)                |
| **Source**  | [Golden Hive Mead — Starter Kit Guide](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0831/9493/7643/files/Starter_Kit_Guide_6a87fdf8-790c-44a3-9d22-23d8dfaf4220.pdf?v=1727883900), p. 29        |

## Ingredients

**Day of batching**

- 3 lbs raw wildflower honey
- K1-V1116 yeast or similar
- Fermaid-O nutrient
- Spring water, to top up

**Post-fermentation**

- Stabilizers
- Honey, to taste
- 10 g oak cubes
- ½ tsp tartaric acid
- ½ tsp malic acid

## Steps

1. Sanitize all equipment.
2. Add the honey and water to the fermenter, leaving 2–3 in of headspace.
3. Pitch the yeast, then add the first dose of nutrient.
4. Stir until evenly combined and record the starting gravity. Keep taking readings through fermentation.
5. Fit the airlock and ferment to completion at room temperature, out of direct sunlight — usually 3–4 weeks.
6. Continue the nutrient schedule through the first three days of fermentation.
7. Rack as needed once fermentation finishes.
8. Stabilize, then backsweeten with honey to taste.
9. Stir in the tartaric and malic acid to taste.
10. Add fining agents if needed.
11. Age on the oak cubes, tasting weekly, and pull the oak once the flavor is right.
12. Bottle.

## Notes

- Expect a slow ferment. With no fruit there's little natural nutrient, so traditionals take longer than fruit meads.
- They don't blow out the way fruit meads do, so less headspace is fine here.
- Finishes like a dry white wine without much complexity — the oak and the backsweetening are what give it depth.
- Author's tasting notes: smooth, tangy, light-bodied, full honey profile.
