# Cursed Mead

Soda fermented with honey. The guide's own words: soda was not meant to be fermented, attempt at your own risk.

|             |                                                          |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Vessel**  | 1 gal fermenter, 2–3 in headspace                        |
| **Yeast**   | K1-V1116 or similar                                      |
| **Primary** | 3–4 weeks, often longer                                  |
| **Must pH** | 3.7–4.0, never above 4.0                                 |
| **Source**  | [Golden Hive Mead — Starter Kit Guide](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0831/9493/7643/files/Starter_Kit_Guide_6a87fdf8-790c-44a3-9d22-23d8dfaf4220.pdf?v=1727883900), p. 33        |

## Ingredients

**Day of batching**

- 2 lbs raw wildflower honey
- K1-V1116 yeast or similar
- Fermaid-O nutrient
- Soda of your choice, to top up
- Baking soda or potassium carbonate

**Post-fermentation**

- Stabilizers
- Honey, to taste

## Steps

1. Sanitize all equipment.
2. Add the honey and soda to the fermenter, leaving 2–3 in of headspace.
3. Measure the pH of the must. Below 3.7, add baking soda 1 g at a time until it sits between 3.7 and 4.0. Do not exceed 4.0.
4. Pitch the yeast, then add the first dose of nutrient.
5. Stir until evenly combined and record the starting gravity. Keep taking readings through fermentation.
6. Fit the airlock and ferment to completion at room temperature, out of direct sunlight.
7. Continue the nutrient schedule through the first three days of fermentation.
8. Rack as needed once fermentation finishes.
9. Stabilize, then backsweeten with honey to taste.
10. Add fining agents if needed.
11. Age to taste, then bottle.

## Notes

- **Stay inside the 3.7–4.0 pH range.** Some acidity is needed to hold back microbial growth while there's no alcohol yet; over-alkalizing can be dangerous.
- Preservative-heavy sodas may ferment slowly or not at all. The author couldn't ferment Monster this way without first building a yeast starter over several days.
- Experimental by design — the guide makes no promise the result is drinkable.
