# Elderberry Mead

Wildflower and buckwheat honey with elderberries, lemongrass and black tea. Dark, jammy, tannic.

|             |                                                          |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Vessel**  | 1 gal fermenter, 2–3 in headspace                        |
| **Yeast**   | K1-V1116 or similar                                      |
| **Primary** | 3–4 weeks                                                |
| **Aged**    | 6 months                                                 |
| **OG → FG** | 1.116 → 1.003, 14.8% ABV (author's batch)                |
| **Source**  | [Golden Hive Mead — Starter Kit Guide](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0831/9493/7643/files/Starter_Kit_Guide_6a87fdf8-790c-44a3-9d22-23d8dfaf4220.pdf?v=1727883900), p. 32        |

## Ingredients

**Day of batching**

- 1 lb raw wildflower honey
- 2 lbs raw buckwheat honey
- K1-V1116 yeast or similar
- Fermaid-O nutrient
- 70 g elderberries
- 1.5 g lemongrass
- 1.5 g black tea leaves
- Spring water, to top up

**Post-fermentation**

- Stabilizers
- Honey, to taste

## Steps

1. Sanitize all equipment.
2. Add the honey, elderberries, herbs and water to the fermenter, leaving 2–3 in of headspace.
3. Pitch the yeast, then add the first dose of nutrient.
4. Stir until evenly combined and record the starting gravity. Keep taking readings through fermentation.
5. Fit the airlock and ferment to completion at room temperature, out of direct sunlight — usually 3–4 weeks.
6. Continue the nutrient schedule through the first three days of fermentation.
7. Remove the fruit and rack as needed once fermentation finishes.
8. Stabilize, then backsweeten with honey to taste.
9. Add fining agents if needed.
10. Age to taste, then bottle.

## Notes

- **Raw elderberries can be toxic** depending on the variant. Research your source before using them.
- Dried elderberries give more flavor simmered with a splash of water and mashed to a pulp than added dry.
- Author's tasting notes: jammy, earthy, tannic, bitter.
